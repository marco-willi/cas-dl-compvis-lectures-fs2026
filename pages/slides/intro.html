From Classical CV to Deep Learning

High-Dimensional Images

An \(800 \times 600\) RGB image: \(800 \times 600 \times 3 = 1{,}440{,}000\) dims.

  • Slow / memory-heavy for classical ML
  • Doesn’t exploit 2-D structure
  • Sensitive to small changes (rotation, shift)
  • Easy to overfit (features ≈ observations)

Why Deep Learning Wins

  • Automatic features — no SIFT/HOG engineering
  • Hierarchical representations — edges → textures → parts → objects
  • Scales with data + compute
  • End-to-end optimization — pixels → task output
  • Transfer learning — pretrain once, adapt anywhere

Deep Learning History

Hubel & Wiesel (1959)

Figure 37: Simple and complex cells in the cat visual cortex respond to specific patterns (Hubel and Wiesel (1959)).

Neocognitron (1980)

Figure 38: Hierarchical, biology-inspired pattern-recognition network — precursor to modern CNNs (Fukushima (1980)).

Backpropagation (1986)

Figure 39: Gradient-based training of neural networks via the chain rule (Rumelhart et al. (1986)).

A good intro: 3Blue1Brown — Backpropagation Calculus

LeNet-5 (1998)

Figure 40: First successful CNN — handwritten digit recognition (Lecun et al. (1998)).

After AlexNet — Modern CNNs

  • VGGNet (2014) — depth matters; 16–19 layers, small 3×3 filters
  • ResNet (2015) — skip connections enable 50–152 layers
  • COCO — multi-task benchmarks (detection, segmentation, keypoints)
  • Mobile architectures — efficient on phones / embedded

CNNs vs. Transformers Today

  • CNNs — still widely used; parameter-efficient
  • Transformers — strong raw performance, especially at scale (Dosovitskiy et al. (2020))
  • MLP-based alternatives also competitive (Liu et al. (2021))
  • Hybrids combine the strengths of both (Vasu et al. (2023))
  • Architecture choice often depends on efficiency vs. peak performance (Siméoni et al. (2025))

Both perform similarly in many settings (Smith et al. (2023)) — the question is open.
